## Notes for the post

- The film is an artistic visualisation: the galaxy of threads, the honeycomb, the "dial" of the market and the hourglass
  tree are drawn to explain, not to scale or anatomy.
- "Laid end to end… a tenth of the way across the galaxy" counts only one group of fungi (arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi)
  in topsoil, a modelled estimate (about 110 quadrillion km against a ~100,000-light-year galaxy). The real total is
  larger, since other mycorrhizal fungi aren't counted.
- "Trees talk… old trees feed their young" is presented as a popular story that scientists dispute, not as fact.
- "A third of our fossil fuel emissions" is carbon that flows to the fungi each year, at least for a while; how long it
  stays underground is not known.

## Facts and where they come from (checked 2026-11-28)

- **Thread length:** about 110 quadrillion km of arbuscular mycorrhizal hyphae in the world's topsoils (Stewart et al.,
  *Science*, June 2026; SPUN, University of Sheffield, AMOLF). https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1131131,
  https://phys.org/news/2026-06-global-mycorrhizal-fungi-reveals-true.html. Milky Way ~100,000 light years ≈ 9.5 × 10¹⁷
  km, so 110 × 10¹⁵ km ≈ 0.12 of its width.
- **400 million years:** a mycorrhiza fossil in the early land plant *Aglaophyton*, which had no roots, from the 407-million-year-old
  Windyfield Chert, Scotland (New Phytologist, 2025; https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2025/november/ancient-fossil-reveals-how-plants-fungi-first-developed-land.html).
  Arbuscules in the ~400-million-year-old Rhynie chert: Remy, Taylor et al., 1994/1995. Fungi and plants have traded
  for "nearly 450 million years" (Oyarte Gálvez et al., Nature 2025).
- **"Little trees":** arbuscule comes from the Latin *arbusculum*, a small tree or shrub; arbuscules branch inside root cells.
- **Sugar for minerals, dissolving stone:** plants pass carbon (sugars, fats) to the fungi; the fungi pass phosphorus,
  nitrogen and water. Ectomycorrhizal fungi tunnel into feldspar and hornblende grains with organic acids (Jongmans
  et al., Nature 389, 682–683, 1997).
- **Both sides keep score:** plants give more carbon to fungi that supply more phosphorus, and fungi give more phosphorus
  to plants that supply more carbon (Kiers et al., Science 333, 880–882, 2011).
- **Two-way rivers, spreading like a wave:** fluid flows both ways at once inside the hyphae; the network grows outward as
  a travelling wave and adds loops that shorten the way to new partners (Oyarte Gálvez et al., "A travelling-wave
  strategy for plant–fungal trade", Nature 639, 172–180, 2025).
- **Nineteen trees, hubs:** in an old-growth interior Douglas-fir forest, single genets of *Rhizopogon* fungi each
  colonised up to 19 trees; big old trees were the most connected hubs and young trees grew within their network
  (Beiler et al., New Phytologist 185, 543–553, 2010).
- **A third of fossil fuel emissions:** plants allocate about 13.12 Gt CO₂e a year to mycorrhizal fungi, ~36% of annual
  fossil fuel CO₂ emissions (Hawkins et al., Current Biology, 2023).
- **The doubt:** Karst, Jones & Hoeksema, "Positive citation bias and overinterpreted results lead to misinformation on
  common mycorrhizal networks in forests", Nature Ecology & Evolution 7, 501–511 (2023), found no peer-reviewed field
  study showing adult trees preferentially send resources or warnings to young trees; Simard, Ryan & Perry replied in
  Frontiers in Forests and Global Change (2025).
- **More than nine in ten:** about 8% of flowering plant species are non-mycorrhizal (Brundrett & Tedersoo, New
  Phytologist 220, 1108–1115, 2018).
