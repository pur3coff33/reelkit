## Notes for the post

- The film is an artistic visualisation; quarks, the nucleus and the cosmic web are drawn to be understood, not to scale.
- "Where space itself boils" (quantum foam at the Planck scale) is a hypothesis, said as fact for the mood.
- "Exactly in the middle" depends on taking the Planck length as the smallest size and the observable universe as the
  largest (the film says "the edge of all we can see").
- "Look out, and it never ends. Look in, and it never ends." is poetry, not physics: the observable universe has an edge
  and below the Planck length size may stop meaning anything.

## Facts and where they come from (checked against sources, 2026-11-25)

- **The middle:** log-scale midpoint of the Planck length (1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m, CODATA) and the observable universe's diameter (~8.8 × 10²⁶ m, 93 Gly) is 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ m (0.12 mm; 0.084 mm if you use the radius). A mature human egg is 110–120 µm across (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6992407/).
- **Empty space:** "pairs of particles flicker into being" is the popular picture of vacuum fluctuations, whose effects (Casimir, Lamb shift) are measured.
- **Proton mass:** the quarks' own rest mass is about 1%; the rest is QCD energy: quark motion ~32%, gluon field ~37%, trace anomaly ~23%, condensate ~9% (APS Physics 11, 118, https://physics.aps.org/articles/v11/118; breakdown from Yang et al., PRL 121, 212001, 2018).
- **Nucleus vs atom:** nucleus ~10⁻¹⁵–10⁻¹⁴ m, atom ~10⁻¹⁰ m, about 1/100,000 the width (the "seed in a stadium" picture).
- **Human genome:** about 3 billion base pairs (NHGRI).
- **Cells in a body:** about 30–37 trillion (Sender et al., PLoS Biol 2016; Bianconi et al., 2013).
- **People:** about 8.2 billion (UN World Population Prospects 2024).
- **Iron in blood:** made in supernova explosions; roughly half to two thirds of the Sun's iron comes from Type Ia (exploding white dwarfs), the rest from massive stars collapsing.
- **Milky Way:** 100–400 billion stars (NASA).
- **Cosmic web:** spans the observable universe (~93 billion light years), far more than light has crossed in 13.8 billion years.
