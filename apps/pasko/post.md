# Seven Plates — post notes

1:00, 9:16, no voice. One Filipino yard, one Christmas Eve a year from 1998 to 2026. A family of seven eats Noche Buena
at a long table under the parol. One by one the five children leave home (abroad as a nurse, to sea, to marry, to work
in Dubai, to Canada), and a balikbayan box sits on each empty chair. In 2026 the old parents still set seven plates,
light the parol and leave the gate open. A light comes down the road. Who it is, the film doesn't say.

## Notes for the post

- Fiction: an original story, drawn and animated in code in a low-poly style. Nobody real is shown.
- Nobody dies in the film. The children leave home for work and family, as many Filipino families know.
- The music is original, played on sampled real instruments (VSCO 2 Community Edition, CC0). In 1998 it is played
  like a rondalla (plucked strings in tremolo); no existing carol is used.
- It is one unbroken camera move: the years melt into each other around it, and each child who leaves fades into the
  balikbayan box on their chair.
- The yard's sounds (spoons, chairs, crickets, the tuko, church bells, the jeepney, the tricycle, the capiz chimes, the
  neighbour's videoke) are synthesised in code too.
- Things viewers may miss, good for replies: the table gets quieter every year (fewer spoons on plates, until two);
  the neighbour's videoke sings the film's own tune; each plate Nanay sets in 2026 plays one note of the theme; the tuko
  calls seven times; the theme never comes home at the end; the gate is left open; a tricycle comes down the road.
