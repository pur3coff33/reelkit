## Notes

- **What's real and what's drawn.** The photographs are real, taken in 1991 by USGS, the U.S. Air Force, the U.S. Navy and NOAA. The NASA globes are a satellite data graphic covering 1991–94. The maps, the height diagram and the title card were drawn for this video.
- **The maps and diagram are to scale, but simplified:**
  - The height diagram is to scale. The column's shape is illustrative.
  - Typhoon Yunya's track follows the JMA best track (Digital Typhoon, NII).
  - The ash cloud's 400 km width is from *Fire and Mud*; its drawn shape is illustrative.
  - The Central Luzon coastline on the evacuation map is approximate.
- **Sources** for every figure in the film:
  - USGS Fact Sheet 113-97, *The Cataclysmic 1991 Eruption of Mount Pinatubo* (https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/1997/fs113-97/). Covers: second-largest of the century, the 500-year slumber, the craters blasted in April, 35 km, the roofs, nearly 20 million tons of SO₂, about 0.5 °C of cooling from 1991 through 1993, at least 5,000 lives saved.
  - USGS Fact Sheet 115-97 (https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/1997/fs115-97/). Covers: the 10 km danger zone, lives saved.
  - Wolfe & Hoblitt, *Overview of the Eruptions*, in *Fire and Mud* (https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/wolfe/). Covers:
    - alert level 5 on June 9, and the 20 km and 30 km evacuation zones;
    - about 14,500 people leaving Clark on June 10, and at least 58,000 evacuees by June 12;
    - Typhoon Yunya and the 400 km ash cloud;
    - 200 to 300 deaths ("hundreds").
  - NASA Earth Observatory, *Global Effects of Mount Pinatubo* (https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/global-effects-of-mount-pinatubo-1510). Covers: the stratosphere starting at about 10 km, and cooling "for a period of almost two years". NASA gives 15 million tons and 0.6 °C; the film uses the USGS figures.
  - JMA best track for Typhoon 199105 (Yunya), via Digital Typhoon (https://agora.ex.nii.ac.jp/digital-typhoon/summary/wnp/l/199105.html.en). Covers: the typhoon's path across Luzon.
  - Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Pinatubo (https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=273083). Covers: the volcano's location.
- **Rounded or chosen figures:** "about half a degree" (USGS 0.5 °C; NASA 0.6 °C); "about 14,500" from Clark (Wolfe, June 10; FS115 says more than 15,000 before June 15).
